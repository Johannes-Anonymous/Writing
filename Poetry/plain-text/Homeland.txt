# Homeland

The woman appeared during the night,
in the blackness that can only come
when the moon is gone.

In the frosted morning we told her our names,
walked through our fields together,
and shared our apples, walnuts, and cherries —
though many were rotten,
some were still colorful and ripe.

We shared the story of our orchards, the trees,
and how they took shape from our lives,
with both gnarled branches and majestic limbs.

As she listened,
she warmed our hands,
rubbing them between her palms.

Then she led us to the harbor,
whence she came,
and asked if we would finally name our ships.

Built last spring, they had sat moored, unused,
yet rigged and bare to the sea,
their sails salty and torn in the rugged wind.
Ice had scarred their hulls.

We spoke in confusion, nearly a ruckus,
until my youngest boy stepped forward,
and walking to the largest sailer,
bowed and pronounced it “Lily Rose,”
declaring it noble and delicate.

In that foggy blue hour,
with our breath like steam,
we knew he was right,
and the silence of agreement came over us like a cloak.

We stood still on the pier,
gazing upon the horizon with a new wind in our hair,
until the ravens began to cry.

Then, we prepared for our departure.
