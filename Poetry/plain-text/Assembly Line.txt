# Assembly Line

Ten-hour shifts filled up my summer with sweat —
    Oh, I remember those nights.

Soaking my shirt under fluorescent lights —
    Oh, by toil and pain, I learned a lot.


    I learned how metal sheets cut my hands,
        how quotas stay the same.

    I learned to spot dead eyes in another's face,
        to not bother asking for a name.

    I learned our lives were as empty as my lunchbox,
        walking home at 3 A.M., indifferent to the rain.

    I learned that sunshine doesn't matter,
        when you sleep through most of the day.

    I learned to spot death in my own mirror,
        to treat each night like it might be my grave.

    And waking up, I would shrivel in the shower,
        without caring if I came out clean.


In the end, I stood naked and cold,

    just wasted flesh,

        wracked by pain.
