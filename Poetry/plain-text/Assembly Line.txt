# Assembly Line

Ten-hour shifts filled up my summer with sweat —
    Oh, I remember those nights.

Soaking my shirt under fluorescent lights —
    Oh, from toil and pain, I was taught.

I saw metal sheets cut my hands,
    and quotas stay the same.

I learned to spot dead eyes in another's face,
    to not bother asking for their name.

I was taught our lives were as empty as my lunchbox,
    walking home at 3 A.M., indifferent to the rain.

I learned that sunshine doesn't matter,
    when you sleep through most of the day.

And I learned to spot death in the mirror,
    to treat each night like another grave.

After bed, I shriveled in the shower,
    not caring if I came out clean.

I stood naked and cold before my end,
    and learned to call myself “waste.”
